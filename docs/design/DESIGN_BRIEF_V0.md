# Listen Closer visual exploration brief — V0

Status: **fixed research brief, not selected art direction**  
Owner: #1143

This brief exists so different design workflows can be compared against the **same problem**. Do not rewrite the brief to suit the tool being evaluated.

## Product in one sentence

Listen Closer helps a musically curious person turn a recording into something they can hear, inspect, understand, question, and compare without losing their place in the music.

## What the brand should make someone feel

These are target qualities, not aesthetic prescriptions:

- **curious** — invites closer listening rather than presenting a verdict;
- **precise** — evidence and state feel considered, not fuzzy or magical;
- **musical** — belongs around music without relying on cliché notation/equalizer decoration;
- **intelligent** — sophisticated without looking like enterprise analytics;
- **human** — explanation and discovery feel authored/considered rather than machine-generated filler;
- **contemporary** — feels current without chasing the current AI-design trend;
- **memorable** — there is an ownable visual idea beyond `clean dark SaaS`.

## What the brand should not feel like

- generic AI assistant;
- developer observability dashboard;
- crypto/fintech landing page;
- DAW clone;
- generic `premium audio` black + gold product;
- music-school worksheet;
- music-note/equalizer clip-art app;
- playful toy that weakens trust;
- scientific visualization tool that requires expertise to approach;
- collection of fashionable community components.

## Core product truth

The product's distinctive value is not `AI analyzes music`.

The stronger product story is:

```text
music I care about
→ keep my place while I listen
→ see different truthful representations
→ inspect a supported musical claim
→ hear where it comes from
→ ask / compare / learn from it
```

Art direction may abstract this. It must not fabricate capabilities or data.

## Primary audience for visual evaluation

A musically curious user who cares enough about a recording to inspect it closely, but does not necessarily know MIR terminology or want a professional DAW.

This includes musicians, students/composers/producers and serious listeners. Do not design only for technical MIR experts or only for casual streaming users.

## Surface 1 — signed-out landing

### Job

In a few seconds, communicate that Listen Closer is a distinctive product for **understanding music through listening + inspectable evidence**, then make trying/signing in obvious.

### Required

- product name / identity;
- one dominant proposition;
- one short supporting explanation;
- one primary auth/try CTA;
- privacy/trust support where current product requires it;
- enough product-specific visual material that the page does not read as an auth card or generic AI landing;
- desktop and phone compositions;
- reduced-motion equivalent.

### Forbidden

- fake measured waveform/note/score/finding content presented as if generated by the product;
- fake testimonials, usage numbers or customer logos;
- generic three-feature-card section as the primary story;
- gratuitous shader/particle/aurora merely to signal `AI`;
- copy whose main claim is `AI-powered`.

### Freedom

The landing is allowed to be much more expressive than the signed-in workspace. Typography, illustration, real product output when lawful, editorial composition, photography, abstraction, bold color, texture and motion are all available if they serve the direction.

## Surface 2 — populated workspace

### Job

Feel like a focused instrument for listening and understanding one Work. The music / active representation is the main object. Library, transport, representation switching, selection, Breakdown and Ask must form one coherent hierarchy.

### Required

- canvas/representation remains visually dominant;
- global transport remains easy to find;
- current Work / source / selection state remains understandable;
- inspector content supports real density without card soup;
- playback, selection and evidence emphasis remain distinguishable;
- controls stay usable at constrained laptop width;
- the visual system must tolerate Score's paper/light surface and dark/graphical representations without looking like separate products.

### Forbidden

- giant decorative brand element competing with music;
- pervasive gradients/glow around ordinary controls;
- every section becoming a rounded card;
- decorative color that looks like semantic evidence/playback state;
- workspace redesign that changes product semantics merely to fit the concept.

### Freedom

The workspace does **not** have to stay graphite, warm-neutral, or dark. Light, mixed, colored, editorial or technical directions are legitimate research candidates if the hierarchy and content still work.

## Surface 3 — empty / first-use workspace

### Job

Make `Import a recording` unmistakable while giving a first hint of product identity and what will become possible.

### Required

- one primary import action;
- no invented musical evidence/state;
- connection to the selected brand direction;
- substantially calmer than a marketing hero.

Historical `shared-time scaffold` is one possible treatment, not a requirement.

## Surface 4 — favicon / small mark

### Job

Be identifiable as Listen Closer at browser/app-icon scale.

### Required tests

- 16 px;
- 32 px;
- 64 px;
- monochrome silhouette;
- dark and light background sanity;
- recognizable without surrounding wordmark.

### Avoid by default

- generic waveform;
- equalizer bars;
- music note;
- sparkle/AI star;
- play triangle;
- headphone silhouette;
- micro-detailed `multiple representations` infographic compressed into an icon.

A familiar motif may still be used only if transformed into something genuinely ownable.

## Copy constraints

Current generic descriptive language should not be assumed final.

The selected direction should include copy experiments for:

- landing headline;
- landing supporting line;
- primary CTA;
- empty-workspace explanation;
- one Breakdown finding presentation.

Copy should be specific to the user's musical progress. Avoid:

- `unlock insights`;
- `AI-powered` as primary differentiation;
- `supercharge` / `revolutionize` / `next-generation`;
- empty claims such as `understand music like never before`;
- excessive internal vocabulary like Work, Version, Artifact, evidence projection.

## Fixed comparison content

When possible, candidate visual directions should render **identical semantic content** so different wording/data does not bias aesthetic comparison.

### Landing semantic payload

Headline intent:

> Hear more in the music you care about.

Supporting intent:

> Move through a recording, notes, notation and evidence without losing your place—and inspect why a musical explanation is supported.

These are *semantic placeholders*, not approved brand copy. A direction may rewrite them while preserving the factual promise.

Primary action intent:

> Try Listen Closer / Continue with Google

### Empty state semantic payload

> Import a recording.

> Listen, inspect, select and understand it in one place.

Again, wording may vary as long as the meaning stays fixed.

## Evaluation surfaces

Every serious direction should eventually provide:

```text
landing / 1440×900
landing / 390×844
workspace populated / 1440×900
workspace constrained / ~1024px wide
empty workspace / 1440×900
favicon / 16px, 32px, 64px
reduced-motion landing
```

Workspace direction prototypes may initially use captured/current content rather than functioning production code, but final comparison must use real product density.

## Required divergence

A direction is not distinct merely because it changes:

- accent color;
- font family;
- border radius;
- background gradient.

At least four of these must materially differ across candidate directions:

- composition;
- typography hierarchy;
- material/surface model;
- color strategy;
- geometry/icon language;
- relationship between marketing and workspace;
- product visualization treatment;
- copy voice;
- motion character;
- density / whitespace strategy.

## Critique questions

Before presenting a direction, answer:

1. If the Listen Closer logo disappeared, what makes this product visually identifiable?
2. Which single visual idea is most memorable?
3. Which part looks most like a generated template?
4. Which choice is purely decorative and has not earned its cost?
5. Does the workspace still feel good after 30 minutes, not only in a hero screenshot?
6. Does Score look integrated rather than embedded from another product?
7. Is the first useful action more obvious than before?
8. Could this direction support future Compare / richer evidence without visual collapse?
9. What does this direction intentionally *not* emphasize?
10. What would make us reject it after seeing the rendered product states?

## Research success condition

The goal of the first exploration round is **not** to obtain approval for a final brand.

It succeeds when the product owner can compare several credible, materially different answers to:

> What should a beautiful, ownable Listen Closer feel like?

and give directional feedback based on rendered evidence rather than trying to invent the brand from abstract adjectives alone.
